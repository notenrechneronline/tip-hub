Food Delivery Tipping: DoorDash, Uber Eats & Pizza — What's Fair

Delivery drivers pay for their own gas, car, and time — the app's fees do not go to them. The rule is simple: 20% or $5, whichever is more. Here is how it plays out across DoorDash, Uber Eats, and old-school pizza delivery.

Quick answer Tip 20% of the food subtotal, with a $5 minimum — even on small orders. Add $2–3 in bad weather or for long distances. The tip goes to the driver; the app's service and delivery fees do not.

The 20% or $5 rule

Percentages break down on small orders: 20% of a $12 burrito is $2.40, which does not cover a driver's trip. Hence the floor. Worked example: a $32 DoorDash order × 20% = $6.40 — round to $7. Punch it into a DoorDash tip calculator and you will see the same number in one tap.

Small orders deserve the minimum, not the percentage

A driver accepts your $11 taco order, drives 15 minutes, climbs your stairs — for $2.28 at 20%? The $5 minimum exists precisely for this. If the order is under $25, just tip $5 and do not do the math. A food delivery tip calculator bakes the minimum right in.

Bad weather, long distances, big orders

Rain, snow, and 100-degree heat make the job meaningfully harder and slower. Add $2–3 on top of your normal tip — it is the highest-impact tipping upgrade there is, because most customers do not do it. For distances over ~5 miles or orders over $75, scale up similarly: the driver's costs (gas, time) scale with both.

App fees are not the tip

This is the most expensive misunderstanding in food delivery. The "service fee," "delivery fee," and "small order fee" on your receipt go to the app, not the driver. The driver gets base pay (a few dollars) plus your tip. Tipping in the app is real; assuming the fees covered it is not. The same split applies to restaurant takeout ordered through third-party apps.

Pizza delivery specifics

Pizza shops often run their own drivers (not gig-app contractors), and the etiquette is identical: 20% or $5 minimum. Two pizza-specific notes: a pizza tip calculator is handy because pizza math is per-pie and people under-tip multi-pizza orders; and if the shop charges a "delivery fee," that fee stays with the shop — tip on top of it.

Do not tip-bait: putting a big tip to get your order accepted fast, then lowering it after delivery, gets drivers to blacklist your address. Tip what you mean the first time.

Cash vs in-app

In-app is fine and, importantly, it is counted: drivers see the offered total before accepting, so a good in-app tip gets your food picked up faster. Cash on delivery works too, but the driver cannot see it in advance. Either way, never mark "no tip, will tip cash" and then forget — drivers remember addresses.

Frequently asked questions

Should I tip if the delivery was late?

If the restaurant was slow, yes — tip normally; the driver waited too. If the driver was clearly joyriding with your food going cold, dropping to 10–15% is fair.

Do I tip on the total with fees included?

No — tip on the food subtotal before fees and tax. Fees do not feed the driver, so they should not inflate the tip base either.

What about grocery delivery?

Same rule: 20% or $5 minimum, more for heavy orders (cases of water, 20 bags). Shoppers do real physical labor.

Is it okay to tip in cash to avoid app fees?

Cash is fine, but in-app tips help you twice: the driver sees a good offer and accepts faster. A hybrid — solid in-app tip — is the practical winner.
