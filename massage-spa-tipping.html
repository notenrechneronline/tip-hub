Massage & Spa Tipping: How Much to Tip Your Therapist

A massage should end with relaxation, not math anxiety. The standard is 20% on the pre-tax service price — here is how it works across day spas, resort spas, packages, and the confusing "service charge" line.

Quick answer Tip 20% of the pre-tax service price. A $120 deep-tissue massage gets $24. Tip on the full value of packages (not the discounted price), and always ask whether a "service charge" actually goes to your therapist.

The 20% standard, with real numbers

Worked example: $120 deep tissue × 20% = $24. Run it through a massage tip calculator when the bill stacks a massage, facial, and scrub — the combined total surprises people.

The service-charge trap

The most expensive misunderstanding in spa tipping: a service charge on your bill is not necessarily a tip. Many resort and hotel spas add 18–22% that goes partly or entirely to the house. Always ask before you pay: "Does the service charge go to my therapist, or should I add a gratuity?" If it does not reach your therapist, add your 20% on top.

Packages: tip on the full value

Bought a $220 three-service package advertised at $160? Tip on the $220 pre-discount value. The discount was between you and the business; the gratuity reflects the work actually performed. Same rule for gift cards and Groupon-style vouchers — and for a very similar reason, TipCalculatorPro's free calculator is handy when the receipt math gets murky.

Resort and hotel spas

Resort spas add service charges more often than neighborhood day spas, and some add them automatically to every treatment. When the charge does not go to the therapist, tip on top at the standard 20%. If you see the same therapist across a multi-day stay, consistent 20% tips build a real relationship — therapists remember.

Medical spas: at med spas (Botox, laser, clinical facials), tipping is genuinely optional — the providers are often salaried clinicians. 10–15% is a nice gesture for the aesthetician's portion, but nobody expects 20% on a $600 laser package.

Who gets the tip (and who does not)

Tip the hands-on practitioners: massage therapist, esthetician, nail tech. You do not need to tip the front desk, the locker attendant, or the person who handed you cucumber water. One exception: if a single assistant spent real time with you (a 30-minute scalp massage add-on), a few dollars directly to them is right. The same "tip the worker, not the room" principle applies at the nail salon and hair salon.

Frequently asked questions

Do I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax total?

Pre-tax, on the service subtotal. Tax goes to the government, not your therapist.

The spa added a 20% service charge. Do I add more?

Ask where it goes. If the spa confirms it reaches your therapist as their gratuity, you are done. If it goes to the house — or the answer is vague — add your own 20%.

Do I tip the owner if they do my massage?

Modern practice says yes, the same 20%. The old custom against tipping owners has faded.

Cash or card for the tip?

Both work, but cash is preferred by most therapists. If you paid with a gift card, bring cash or a card for the gratuity.
