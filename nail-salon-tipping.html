Nail Salon Tipping: Manicures, Pedicures & Acrylics

Nail techs do detailed, physical work inches from your hands for an hour at a time. The baseline is 20% — here is how it scales across manicures, pedicures, acrylics, and the situations people always ask about.

Quick answer Tip 20% of the service total. A $45 gel manicure gets $9; a $70 spa pedicure gets $14. Tip the owner the same as anyone else, and add a little extra when one tech does both your mani and pedi back-to-back.

The 20% baseline, by service

Worked example: a $45 gel manicure × 20% = $9. Check it on a nail salon tip calculator when you are stacking services — the totals climb faster than people expect.

Manicure vs pedicure vs acrylics

Price differences reflect time and skill, so the percentage handles it automatically — but two nuances matter. First, acrylics and extensions take 60–90 minutes of meticulous work; if your tech nailed a complex set, 22–25% is a fair upgrade. Second, soak-offs: if removal is itemized ($10–15), tip on the full bill including it. That scraping is real labor.

Do you tip the salon owner?

Yes — same 20% as anyone else. The old "never tip the owner" rule is dead in practice, and owners who do nails full-time earn like technicians. Withholding the tip reads as a statement, not savings.

Group appointments and bridal parties

For groups of 4+, ask whether gratuity is auto-added — many salons add 18–20% for parties, just like restaurants. If it is, do not double-tip; if it is not, make sure everyone in the group actually tips. The maid of honor should confirm, not assume. Similar group math applies at the hair salon, where the salon tip calculator logic is identical: percentage on the pre-tax total, cash preferred.

One tech, two services: when the same technician does your manicure and pedicure back-to-back (nearly two hours), bump to 22–25%. They just gave you half their afternoon.

Cash, card, and the front desk

Cash handed directly to your tech is ideal. On card is fine. What to skip: leaving cash "at the front desk for everyone" — pooled front-desk tips rarely reach the right hands in full. The etiquette mirrors spa tipping: tip the hands-on worker, not the room.

Frequently asked questions

How much do you tip for a $35 manicure?

$7 at 20%. Round to $7–8; nail techs notice round-ups and remember generous regulars at booking time.

Do I tip if the polish chips the next day?

Go back first — reputable salons fix chips free within a few days. Tip on the redo as if you paid for it; the tech is still doing the work.

Should I tip on a gift-card appointment?

Yes, on the full service value, paid separately in cash or on your own card. The gift card covers the service; the tip is still yours to give.

Is 15% ever okay at a nail salon?

For a rushed, visibly careless job, 15% is fair. For a language-barrier misunderstanding rather than carelessness, stay at 20% — effort was still made.
