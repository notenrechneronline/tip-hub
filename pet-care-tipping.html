Do You Tip Dog Groomers? How Much (15–20%)

Yes, you tip your dog groomer. They wrestle a living animal through bath, cut, and nails — often while getting bitten. 15–20% is the standard, scaled to your dog's difficulty level.

Quick answer Tip 15–20% of the grooming bill. Easy small dog: 15%. Large, matted, or dramatic dog: 20%+. A $75 groom at 20% is $15 — run it on a dog groomer tip calculator if you are stacking de-shedding and nail grinding.

The 15–20% sliding scale

Unlike a haircut, the "difficulty" variable is huge — a calm Shih Tzu versus a 90-pound husky mid-blowout are different jobs. Scale honestly:

Small vs large dogs

Large dogs take twice the shampoo, twice the drying time, and four times the wrestling. If your Newfoundland's groom costs the same as a terrier's, the tip is where you correct the imbalance — 20%+ on big dogs is the norm among groomers, and they absolutely track which giant-breed owners tip fairly.

Matted coats and "difficult" dogs

Dematting is painful, slow, skilled work — and groomers will tell you it is the hardest part of the job. If your dog arrived matted, 20–25% acknowledges that reality. Same for biters, screamers, and seniors who need extra-gentle handling. And the kindest long-term move: keep a regular schedule so matting never happens; it is cheaper than the dematting surcharge and the guilt tip.

Mobile groomers

Mobile groomers drive a salon to your driveway — tip 20% minimum. They have van costs, fuel, and no walk-in volume to smooth the day. Many mobile groomers are solo operators; your tip is a large share of their margin. The economics resemble home-service pros more than salon staff.

Holiday tips: for a regular groomer, the year-end bonus is one full groom's price — a $65 groom means $65 extra in December. Groomers remember holiday tippers all year, and appointment flexibility follows.

What about vets, walkers, and boarders?

Vets: no tip expected (they are medical professionals); a holiday card or small gift for a beloved vet is lovely but optional. Dog walkers: 15–20% per walk, or a holiday bonus of one week's fees. Boarding: 10–15% of the stay, more if meds were administered. The throughline matches personal-care tipping: tip the hands-on caregiver, at 15–20%, in cash when you can. Frequently asked questions

Do I tip if my dog was badly behaved?

Especially then — 20%+. The groomer earned hazard pay. Apologize, tip well, and work on the behavior (or warn them next time).

Should I tip the salon owner if they groom my dog?

Yes, same 15–20%. The no-tipping-the-owner rule is long dead across personal services.

What about self-serve dog washes?

$3–5 for the attendant who helped with the dryer or cleaned the tub. If you truly did everything solo, nothing is expected.

Is it okay to tip 10% for a quick nail trim?

$3–5 flat is the norm for a $12 nail trim — which works out above 20% anyway. Quick services get flat amounts, not percentages.
