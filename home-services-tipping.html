Do You Tip Plumbers, Electricians & Handymen? The Honest Answer

Here is the honest answer nobody puts on a tip screen: tipping home-service pros is not expected — and genuinely appreciated when you do. This guide covers when a tip makes sense, how much, and the alternatives that matter more.

Quick answer Tipping plumbers, electricians, and handymen is optional. For exceptional work — emergency calls, filthy jobs, above-and-beyond effort — $10–20 or 10% is a strong gesture. Reviews and repeat business often matter more than cash.

The honest rule

Tradespeople bill $75–150+ per hour and set their own rates — unlike servers, their wage does not assume tips. Nobody at the supply house thinks you are cheap for not tipping your plumber. So the baseline is $0 with a clear conscience, and any tip is a bonus for exceptional work, not an obligation. That framing changes everything: tip to reward, never out of guilt.

When to tip 10–20% (or $10–20 flat)

Tip when the job went beyond the invoice:

SituationSuggested tip
Emergency / after-hours call$20–50
Disgusting job done cheerfully (sewage, crawlspace)$20
Finished significantly under estimate10% of savings
Apprentice did great work under supervision$10–15 to the apprentice directly
Handyman half-day, excellent work$15–20

Worked example: a $180 emergency drain clearing on a Sunday — a $20 tip says "I know this ruined your weekend." A plumber tip calculator frames the percentage if you prefer 10% math ($18 on $180).

What about house cleaners?

Cleaners are the exception in home services: 15–20% per visit is the norm for regular cleaners, or a holiday bonus of one full cleaning. They are in your home, handling your things, on a schedule — the relationship economics look like a salon, not a plumber. For one-off deep cleans, 15–20% of the job price.

Holiday tips for regulars

For pros you use regularly — the cleaner, the lawn crew, the handyman on retainer — the year-end bonus is one visit's price. It is tax-deductible-adjacent goodwill that buys you priority scheduling all next year. A card with cash beats a gift card: cash is fungible, gift cards guess wrong.

What matters more than cash

For tradespeople, three things beat a $20 tip: 1. A detailed 5-star review with photos — worth hundreds in future business. 2. Referrals to neighbors and friends. 3. Repeat business and prompt payment. Do these and you are a dream customer with or without the tip. This is also why movers — who are hourly, not rate-setting — sit on the other side of the line: for them, cash is the thing, so a moving tip calculator per-mover math is the right tool.

Do not tip: cable/internet technicians (they are salaried and often forbidden from accepting), appliance delivery crews beyond $5–10 each for stairs, or anyone who quotes a price and then demands a tip to start work — that is a red flag, walk away.

Frequently asked questions

Is it insulting to tip a plumber?

No — offered warmly ("grab lunch on me") it reads as appreciation, not charity. What can feel off is a tiny tip on a huge invoice; $5 on a $2,000 job is worse than nothing. Scale or skip.

Should I tip the apprentice separately?

Yes, directly and in cash. Apprentices earn the least and do the heaviest lifting — $10–15 handed to them personally is never wrong.

Do I tip for a free estimate?

No. Estimates are sales calls. A glass of water and a clear decision (yes or no, promptly) is the courtesy they want.

What about lawn care and snow removal crews?

$10–20 per crew member at the holidays, or 15% on a big one-time job (storm cleanup). Weekly crews: the holiday bonus rule.
