How Much to Tip Movers: The $20–$50 Per Mover Rule

Percentages stop making sense when the bill is $3,000. Movers get tipped per person, per job: $20–50 each for a local move, more for long hauls and miserable conditions. Here is the full breakdown.

Quick answer Tip $20–$30 per mover for a half-day local move, $40–$50 per mover for a full-day move. Long-distance crews get $50+ per mover per end. Hand cash to each mover individually — not one lump sum to the foreman and hope.

The per-mover rule, with real numbers

Worked example: a local 3-bedroom move with 3 movers over 6 hours → 3 × $40 = $120 total. A movers tip calculator scales this for crew size and hours so you are not guessing on moving day.

Local vs long-distance

Long-distance moves often involve two different crews — loading and unloading. Tip each crew separately at the $50+/mover level; the unloading crew did not share in the loading tip. Confirm with the company whether the same team travels, but assume two crews unless told otherwise.

Tip the crew chief the same — then a little more

The foreman gets the same per-mover base as everyone else, plus $10–20 extra if they ran the day well: kept things on schedule, protected your floors, handled the one impossible couch. Do not, however, hand the entire tip pool to the foreman "to distribute." Crews consistently report that pooled handoffs get... unevenly distributed. Cash in each person's hand.

Food and drinks are not the tip (but they help)

Cold water, coffee, and lunch on a full-day move are deeply appreciated — and on hot days, expected as basic hospitality. But they do not replace money. Think of refreshments as the baseline of decency and the cash tip as the actual gratuity. Pizza for the crew plus the per-mover tip is the gold standard; a quick a tip calculator check keeps the crew math straight when you are frazzled on moving day.

When not to tip (or to tip less)

Damaged furniture with no accountability, hours late with no communication, or movers who clearly rushed and broke things — these are reduce-or-skip situations. Document damage with photos first, tip 50% or less, and take it up with the company. Never use a zero tip as your only complaint; the company needs to hear it too. Frequently asked questions

Do I tip movers if the company charges a lot already?

Yes — the company's rate pays for trucks, insurance, and overhead. The crew's take-home is hourly, and the tip is how a hard job becomes a good day.

Should I tip the driver separately from the crew?

On long-distance moves the driver is often the foreman — one generous tip covers both roles. On local moves there is usually no separate driver role.

Is beer an acceptable tip?

As a bonus, sure — a cold six-pack after the truck is unloaded is a classic. As a replacement for cash, no. Also: many company policies forbid crews drinking on the job, so hand it over at the very end.

What about packing-only services?

$10–20 per packer for a few hours of packing. It is skilled, tedious work — your grandmother's china survived because of them.
