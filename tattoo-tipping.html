How Much to Tip a Tattoo Artist: 20% on Ink Explained

A tattoo is permanent art on your body, done by someone enduring hours of focused physical work. The standard is 20–25% — higher than most services, because the skill and stakes are higher too.

Quick answer Tip 20–25% of the tattoo price. Small flash ($100) gets $20–25; a $400 half-day session gets $80–100. Tip per session on multi-session pieces, in cash, handed directly to your artist.

The 20–25% standard

Worked example: a $400 sleeve session × 20% = $80. A tattoo artist tip calculator keeps big-session math honest — at these prices, guessing costs real money.

Large pieces and multi-session work

Big work happens across sessions — tip per session, not once at the end. Your artist did a full day's labor each time; the tip should track the work, not the finale. For a three-session sleeve, three session-sized tips. If a session ran exceptionally long or the artist freehanded major elements, lean to 25%. This is the purest case of tipping more for real effort.

Shop cut vs artist tip

Many artists pay the shop 30–50% of the piece price (booth rent or percentage split). Your tip, handed directly to the artist in cash, bypasses that split entirely — it is the only part of your payment the artist keeps in full. That is precisely why tattoo tips run higher than salon tips: the economics demand it.

Touch-ups

Most artists offer free touch-ups within a reasonable window (a few months) for healing issues. The touch-up itself is free; the tip is not waived — $20–40 depending on the work involved. The artist is still spending an hour on you. If the touch-up is needed because you picked at the scab, tip generously and say thank you.

Deposits: the booking deposit (often $50–100) comes off the final price — it is not the tip. Do not mentally count it as gratuity; it was always your money applied to the piece.

Cash is the rule

Hand cash directly to your artist at the end of the session. Card tips at the shop register can get swept into the shop's revenue and split. A handshake, eye contact, and an envelope or folded bills is the tradition — and artists genuinely remember who tipped well when booking coveted slots. Frequently asked questions

Do you tip on a $50 tiny tattoo?

Yes — $10–15. Shop minimums exist because setup and sterilization cost the artist real time regardless of size.

What if I do not like the tattoo?

Still tip the session, then address the issue separately — most reputable artists will work with you on fixes. Withholding the tip does not communicate "fix this"; a conversation does.

Do I tip the apprentice who helped?

If an apprentice did meaningful work (stencil, setup, shading passes), $10–20 directly to them is a classy move they will never forget.

Is 30% too much?

Never too much for work you will wear forever. 25% is the top of the normal range; beyond that is just generosity, and generosity is never wrong here.
