How Much to Tip Your Hair Stylist: The 20% Rule Explained

Your stylist remembers your formula, your part, and that story about your vacation. The 20% rule is how you return the favor — here is exactly how it works, including the assistant, the owner question, and holiday season.

Quick answer Tip 20% of the pre-tax service total to your stylist. Give the shampoo assistant $3–5 separately in cash. At holiday time, add the cost of one full visit as a year-end bonus for a regular stylist.

The 20% rule with real numbers

Hair pricing varies wildly, so percentages keep things fair. The math: an $85 cut × 20% = $17 tip. Run the numbers on a hair stylist tip calculator if the bill has color, toner, and treatments stacked on it.

Do not forget the assistant

The person who shampooed you, applied your toner, and swept hair for two hours is usually paid hourly — and far less than your stylist. Hand them $3–5 in cash directly. Do not fold it into the card tip and hope it trickles down; it often does not.

Cash vs card

Cash tips are king: no processing fees, no waiting for the salon's pay cycle. If you only have a card, tipping on the card is completely fine — just never leave the tip line blank because you "will Venmo them later." For fiddly split bills, a tipping calculator keeps the card math honest.

Do you tip the salon owner?

Old etiquette said no; modern practice says yes. If the owner cut your hair, tip them the same 20%. The custom against tipping owners has faded, and they will not be offended by a gratuity — they will be confused by its absence.

Holiday tips

If you see the same stylist regularly, the year-end tip is the cost of one visit — your $65-cut stylist gets $65 extra in December, ideally in a card with a note. Assistants get $20–25. This is the single highest-ROI tip of your year: it buys you priority booking through the busy season.

What if you hate the cut?

Still tip 15–20% on the visit, then decide calmly whether to return. Stiffing someone and storming out helps no one; a polite "this isn't quite what I asked for, can we adjust it?" at the chair fixes most problems. If the result is truly botched, that is a when to tip less situation — read that guide before you decide. Similar logic applies at the nail salon and spa, where the service is equally personal.

Frequently asked questions

Do I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax total?

Pre-tax, always. Products you buy at the salon (shampoo, styling cream) are retail — tipping on them is optional, and most people do not.

How much do you tip for a $200 color service?

$40 at 20%. Big color bills produce big tips, which is correct: a three-hour double-process is the most skilled work in the salon.

Should I tip if I am getting a free correction?

Yes — tip on what the service would have cost. The stylist is still spending the hour with you.

Is 15% okay for a quick trim?

It is acceptable, but consider the floor: 20% on a $25 trim is $5. Rounding up to $7–8 for a ten-minute job is a small kindness that gets remembered.
